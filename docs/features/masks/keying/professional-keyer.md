# F-MSK-009 — A professional keyer — uneven screens, hair, shadows, spill and bounce light

## Essence

A real green screen is never one colour: it is brighter in the middle and darker at the edges, wrinkled, and
the subject casts shadows on it; green light bounces onto the subject's shoulders and hair. A professional
keyer pulls a clean edge on fine hair, keeps the shadow the director wanted, removes the green cast from the
subject while keeping skin and hair their own colour — on such a screen, not on a test chart.

## The trick behind it

**1. The uneven screen is divided out by a clean plate, and the clean plate can be generated from the
frame itself.** Nuke's IBK keys each pixel against the screen colour AT THAT PIXEL, taken from a clean plate;
when no clean plate was shot, IBKColour builds one by erasing the subject and filling the hole from the
surrounding screen ("push-pull" averaging over a pyramid). The keyer then sees an even screen everywhere —
which is why one key works in the dark corner and the bright centre at once.

**2. Hair needs two mattes.** A single threshold either eats hair or leaves screen in the holes of the body.
Professional keys combine a hard CORE matte (choked, holes filled — the body) with a soft EDGE matte used only
in a band around the core boundary (hair, motion blur). The final alpha is max(core, edge) inside the band.

**3. A shadow is a ratio, not a colour.** Over the screen, the shadow is where the pixel is darker than the
clean plate at the same place: the ratio luma(pixel)/luma(plate) < 1 is kept as a darkening layer (alpha =
1 − ratio) while the screen itself goes transparent. Spill and bounce light are the dual: the plate's colour
mixed into the subject — removed by the screen's excess and replaced by a neutral or a chosen colour, the
luminance restored so skin and blond hair keep their brightness.

## Pros

- Works on real, uneven, shadowed screens; keeps hair and shadows; deterministic (an algorithm).

## Cons

- Ten-plus passes per frame (plate fill pyramid, mattes, morphology, spill); more parameters than a simple key.

## Verdict

Take it as the owner decided on 2026-11-01: an algorithm, not a neural network, as the base — a screen model
with balance in linear light, a clean plate (generated by push-pull or a chosen frame), core and edge mattes,
shadows as a ratio to the plate, spill and bounce removal with colour restore. A neural matte may come later
as an assist; light wrap waits for a second-layer input.
